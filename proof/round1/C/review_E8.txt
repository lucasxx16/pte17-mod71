Any v∈Q is a² for some nonzero a; multiplying every root of a candidate multiset by a preserves its size, multiplicities, odd-moment zeros and nontriviality, and sends odd-multiplicity square classes U to vU. Therefore E7 accepts one U iff it accepts every U in its Q-orbit. Retaining one representative per orbit preserves existence and each cardinality o. 2. Canonical mask. A finite orbit of integer masks has a unique least integer. If its least nonzero mask had bit0 unset, let j>0 be the least set bit. Cyclic right rotation by j has no wrapped set bits, since all positions below j were zero, and equals the ordinary right shift by j, a strictly smaller positive integer. Contradiction. Thus every least representative contains exponent0, i.e. square class1. Periodic masks pose no exception: they may have fewer than35 distinct rotations, but the minimum mask is still unique as an integer and contains bit0.
